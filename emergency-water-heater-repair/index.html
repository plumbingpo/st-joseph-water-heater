Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • The relief valve on the side of the tank dripping, hissing, or discharging
  • Black soot or heat discoloration around the burner door or draft hood
  • A complete loss of hot water throughout the house
  • Any gas odor around the heater, which means getting out and calling the gas company before calling us
  • Sudden loud knocking or boiling sounds coming from inside the tank
  • A breaker that trips every time the electric heater tries to run
  • Water at the tap that is suddenly scalding hot
  • Water pooling around the base of the heater or spreading across the floor

The Benefits of Professional Emergency Water Heater Repair

A water heater emergency stacks several hazards in one small space. There is water on the floor near a 240-volt circuit on electric models, or near a gas valve, burner, and flue on gas models. There is a tank holding water at high temperature and household pressure. And there is a safety valve that, if it is discharging, is doing so for a reason. Homeowners sometimes try to stop the relief valve by capping it, crank on a leaking fitting until it snaps, or relight a pilot beside a gas smell, and each of those can turn a bad afternoon into something far worse. The safest moves for a homeowner are the simple ones: leave for gas, then shut off water and energy, then call for help.

Professional help is not just about having wrenches. It is about knowing why the failure happened so it does not happen again next month. When a relief valve discharges, we measure static water pressure and look for a missing or failed expansion tank. When a tank floods the floor, we locate the true source, whether it is the valve, a fitting, a connector, or the tank wall. When a gas heater dies, we check the thermocouple or flame sensor, gas pressure, and venting before restarting it. The diagnosis determines everything that follows. Components can be replaced the same visit; a failed tank cannot be patched, and you hear that straight and explain the replacement path.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here are our recommendations:

  • Consider an automatic leak shutoff that stops the cold supply the moment water hits the floor
  • Test the T&P valve annually; a valve that sticks or weeps afterward should be replaced
  • Ask about a drain pan and drain line, especially for heaters in attics, closets, or above finished rooms
  • Annual flushing keeps sediment from causing the rumbling and hot spots that lead to leaks
  • Store chemicals and flammables far from the burner, and keep the area around the heater uncluttered
  • Know where the heater shutoff and the main water valve are, and make sure everyone in the house does too
  • Keep the thermostat near 120F to limit scalding and reduce strain on the tank
