No Surprises

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

Before we start any repair, we find out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians check your heater's age and gallon capacity, the burner or elements, the relief valve, the venting, and any signs of corrosion or seepage. Then you get an honest recommendation and the full price up front, whether the job is replacing a thermocouple or replacing the whole unit. No hidden fees, no last-minute add-ons.

A lot of water heater failures give quiet warnings long before the tank quits. Keep an eye out for discolored or rusty hot water, popping noises when the burner fires, hot water that smells like rotten eggs, or a hot water supply that runs out faster than before. Behind those symptoms are usually heavy sediment, a depleted anode rod, or a failing dip tube. An early service call at the first sign often costs far less than cleaning up after a failed tank.

We also help homeowners plan ahead. Most tank water heaters last roughly 8 to 12 years, and a planned water heater replacement is always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency swap after the tank has already leaked. Our technicians know how hard water and local conditions wear on tanks over time, so we can tell you honestly whether your heater has years left or is living on borrowed time.

Maintenance Plans

Maintenance Plans for St. Joseph Homes

A maintenance visit from St. Joseph Water Heater is more than a quick drain and refill. The technician flushes the sediment, pulls and inspects the anode rod, lifts and tests the relief valve, inspects the burner, flue, and draft on gas models, checks elements and thermostats on electric models, and confirms a safe water temperature. Minor faults get caught while they're inexpensive to correct, and your water heater keeps running at full efficiency.

Every maintenance plan we write is a little different. A tank in its later years gets a closer look than a brand-new heater, a tankless unit on hard water calls for regular descaling to keep its heat exchanger clear, and a large family cycles the tank far harder than a couple does. We set up each visit around your heater, your water, and how your family uses it, so every service keeps the unit efficient and protects the equipment you've already paid for.

Think of maintenance as protection for equipment you've already bought. Each visit helps keep the tank clear of sediment, the anode rod doing its job, and the relief valve in working order, which lowers what you pay to heat water while easing the strain on the burner or elements. For most families, the plan costs far less than an early replacement.

Proper Sizing

Hot Water Sized to Your Family's Demand

When it comes to water heaters, bigger is not automatically better. What matters for a tank is the first-hour rating, how many gallons of hot water it can deliver in one busy hour, while for a tankless unit it's the GPM it can hold at your incoming water temperature. That's why we never just copy the size of the old unit. We look at how many people live in the home, the number of bathrooms, and big draws like a soaking tub or a high-capacity washer.

Needs change over time. A new baby, a teenager, or an in-law suite can outgrow the tank that once kept up just fine. A tankless unit heats water on demand and never runs out as long as the flow stays within its GPM rating, while heat pump water heaters draw far less electricity than a standard electric tank. If you're torn between tank and tankless, or gas and electric, we'll explain the honest pros and cons in installed price, operating cost, venting and gas line needs, and installation work.

Repairs We Handle

The Water Heater Repairs Local Homes Need Most

The majority of service calls we get come down to a handful of familiar problems. The water never gets hot. The gas burner won't light, or it lights and shuts off. The shower goes cold after a few minutes, the tank pops every time the burner fires, or a tankless unit starts flashing an error code. Rusty water, a rotten-egg smell, and a dripping relief valve round out the list. We fix every one of them, always targeting the component that actually failed.

Some failures can wait a day, and some can't. A failed element just means lukewarm showers. A relief valve that discharges, on the other hand, is a safety device doing its job and telling you something is wrong, and a slow leak at the base can soak into flooring before anyone notices. Smell gas? Leave the house and call your gas utility before anything else. Our technicians carry the parts to replace elements, thermostats, thermocouples, gas valves, and relief valves, add a thermal expansion tank when pressure is the culprit, and make leak repairs at fittings and drain valves.
